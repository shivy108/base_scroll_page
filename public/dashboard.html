Why Gold Matters ⓘ
5,000-year store of value

Gold is the oldest financial instrument in human history. Every major civilization has independently converged on it as a unit of wealth. Unlike fiat currency, it can't be printed. Its total supply grows less than 2% per year — making it harder than any government-issued currency.

Real interest rates: the #1 driver

Gold has no yield. When real rates (nominal rate minus inflation) go negative, gold beats bonds — why hold a bond yielding less than inflation when gold holds its real value? When real rates rise, bonds become attractive again and gold loses appeal. This single relationship explains the majority of gold's price moves.

What a rising gold price signals

Gold going up = one or more of: real rates falling, USD weakening, geopolitical risk rising, or institutional flight to safety. Gold going down = risk-on, dollar strengthening, or confidence in the financial system rising. Watch the ratio: gold up + stocks down = fear. Gold up + stocks up = inflation hedge demand. Gold down + stocks up = pure risk-on.

Central bank accumulation since 2022

China, Russia, India, and dozens of other central banks have been quietly buying gold at record pace since 2022 — partly to reduce USD dependency after Russia's FX reserves were frozen. This is structural, not speculative demand. It creates a persistent floor that didn't exist a decade ago.

Gold vs Bitcoin

Both are "hard money" with fixed supply — but gold has 5,000 years of institutional trust and Bitcoin has 15. Central banks hold gold, not BTC. Pension funds hold gold, not BTC (yet). They're not substitutes — they serve different roles. Gold is low-volatility institutional safety. BTC is high-beta digital scarcity. In a portfolio, they complement rather than compete.
